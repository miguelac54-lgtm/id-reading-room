Reading expectations

Read the original paper or relevant guideline subsection first, then return for clinical questions, practical applications, and evidence limitations. A paper marked as completed is remembered locally in this browser; login-based synchronization is not yet enabled.

Guidelines and source articles are educational references, not substitutes for current local antibiograms, stewardship protocols, or attending/pharmacy judgment. Institutional conference links are distributed separately.

Competency-based goals

Six ACGME competency domains, preserved from the original curriculum and streamlined for practical inpatient learning.

01

Patient Care and Procedural Skills

Assess unstable patients promptly; obtain focused exposure, immune-status and antimicrobial histories; perform a targeted examination; interpret the consult question; recognize indications and risks of source-control and diagnostic procedures; coordinate transitions and supervision appropriate to training level.

02

Medical Knowledge

Apply pathophysiology, microbiology, diagnostic stewardship and evidence-based therapy across common inpatient ID syndromes. The linked clinical-topic reading map below supports PGY-specific learning.

03

Practice-Based Learning and Improvement

Use current guidelines, critically appraise evidence, convert patient-driven questions into focused learning, give short evidence-based presentations, and incorporate mid-rotation feedback.

04

Interpersonal and Communication Skills

Present a concise problem representation and consult recommendation; communicate critical microbiology and infection-control findings; counsel patients; coordinate multidisciplinary teams.

05

Professionalism

Demonstrate respectful, ethical and reliable care; protect confidentiality; acknowledge uncertainty and limitations; provide feedback and preserve timely documentation.

06

Systems-Based Practice

Account for stewardship, source control, hand hygiene, infection prevention, cost, safety, microbiology/pharmacy partnership, OPAT handoffs and transitions of care.

Training-level progression

Use the same patients and literature, with increasing independence and evidence appraisal across postgraduate years.

PGY1

Identify infection syndromes and unstable patients; obtain focused histories, examine pertinent findings, choose appropriate initial testing, and explain basic empiric coverage.

PGY2

Refine differential diagnoses, understand comorbidities/immunosuppression, choose targeted therapy and duration, recognize source-control needs, and communicate with consulting services.

PGY3

Independently synthesize complex infections, evaluate treatment failure, supervise handoffs, apply literature limitations, and coordinate multidisciplinary care.

Your ID service

Rotation Expectations & Evaluation

What residents should expect before, during, and at the end of their inpatient ID rotation.

01 · Prepare

Before the Rotation

Contact the supervising ID attending in advance to confirm the meeting time, location, patient assignments, and documentation expectations.

02 · Participate

During the Rotation

Evaluate assigned consults with supervision; review clinical and microbiologic data; present recommendations; document and follow up on patients; coordinate handoffs and attend scheduled teaching when available. Use clinical questions to guide reading.

03 · Reflect

Feedback & Evaluation

Discuss learning goals with faculty, receive formative feedback during the rotation and at mid-rotation when feasible, and review a final evaluation of reasoning, patient care, communication, and documentation.
